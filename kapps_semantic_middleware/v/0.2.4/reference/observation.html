Observation#

What an instance has seen of its connections and of its graph binding, as typed records: mw.connections and mw.graph_binding give them, and is_connection_metadata names the fields that a page without connection metadata leaves out.

What an instance has seen of its connections and of its graph binding, as typed records.

Nothing in the framework or in the connectors records whether a connector is connected, when it last moved a value, or when the store last answered. This module records it, in memory, at two choke points, and adds no traffic of its own:

  • A connection. The instance hands the framework an observing wrapper in place of each connector it wires (_observed_connector). One call site covers REST, MQTT and the knowledge graph connector, and no connector class changes. The MQTT leg is the framework’s own connector, which the dependency policy keeps to bugfixes.

  • The graph binding. Every read and write of the instance reaches the store through its OGM’s db: the OGM’s own methods do, and so do the ~25 direct queries of this package. The instance puts an observing store in that place (_observe_store), once per OGM.

The records are the public read surface: SemanticMiddleware.connections and SemanticMiddleware.graph_binding give them, and a dashboard box and a later exporter read them the same way. A record is frozen and is made when it is read, with this instance’s resource and service IRI attached then. A field that may carry connection metadata, such as an error message that names a host, a port, a topic or a URL, is marked as such in the record’s type, and is_connection_metadata reads the mark. A surface that must not show connection metadata leaves out what that one predicate names, and keeps no list of its own.

The records live as long as the process. A restart empties them.

class kapps_semantic_middleware.observation.ConnectionRecord(resource: IRI | None, service: IRI | None, connector_id: str, holder: Any, field: str, connector: str, direction: str, connected: bool | None, last_provide: datetime | None, last_consume: datetime | None, last_receive: datetime | None, last_error: CallError | None)[source]#

Bases: object

What the instance has seen of one connector that it wired, since it started.

resource and service are this instance’s own IRIs. holder is the individual that holds the parameter, and field the parameter’s field in the datamodel. connector names the connector’s class, and direction the way it moves data.

connected is None until the framework asks the connector to connect, True once its connect() has returned, and False after its disconnect() or a connect() that raised. It is what the connector’s own connect() reports: a connector whose connect() does nothing, such as the knowledge graph connector, is “connected” as soon as it returns.

last_provide is the last provide(), last_consume the last consume() that returned, and last_receive the last value that receive() yielded. All times are aware, in UTC.

property last_sync: datetime | None#

the newer of the last receive and the last consume.

Not provide: a pushing connector, such as the MQTT one, answers provide() from a value it holds and reads nothing, so a fresh provide says nothing of the value’s age.

class kapps_semantic_middleware.observation.GraphBindingRecord(resource: IRI | None, service: IRI | None, dataset: str | None, named_graph: IRI | None, last_read: datetime | None, last_write: datetime | None, last_error: CallError | None)[source]#

Bases: object

What the instance has seen of its graph binding, the store behind its OGM, since it started.

resource and service are this instance’s own IRIs. dataset is the store’s dataset, and named_graph the graph that the instance writes to, None for the default graph.

last_read and last_write are the last read and write that the store answered. A write that the store refused, by a shape or by a false precondition, is an answer: the binding works, and the refusal shows in the instance’s refused writes. last_error is the last call that did not reach the store, or that the store failed itself: no answer, refused credentials or access, or a server error.

Several instances in one process may share one OGM, and so one store. Each sees the same reads, writes and error, which are the store’s, under its own IRIs.
