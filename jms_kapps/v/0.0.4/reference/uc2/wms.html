jms_kapps.uc2.wms#

The WarehouseManagementSystem: where a box’s journey starts, and where it ends.

It stands for the production system level around the FlexConveyor station. It is an active party, with control logic and no conveyor of its own: its own middleware instance with two workflows, :SpawnBoxWorkflow and :SetSpawnRateWorkflow, a handler for the notifications it gets, and a dashboard of its own. It owns the station’s entry.

Spawn. Roles are optional: entry and exit are roles of a module, which a topology may name, and one module may have both. Unless the request names them, the WarehouseManagementSystem picks a random free entry module, read from the graph, as the box’s origin, or a random free module where the topology names no entry module; and a random exit module as its destination, or a random module where it names no exit module. It never picks the origin as the destination. A named origin must be an entry module, and a named destination an exit module, only where the topology names that role; any two different modules otherwise. A spawn that names a module that may not take its place is refused with why, such as “Module4 is not an entry module” or, where the topology names no such role, “Module9 is not a module”. If no such module is free, it spawns nothing and answers why: “no entry module is free”, or “no module is free”. A box is named by a new UUID (vocabulary.new_box_iri), so that more than one middleware can create boxes without a shared number, and the spawn reads no box in the graph to name it.

Only a reachable destination. A destination is reachable from an origin where the connections in the graph lead from the origin to it, each one way, as listed; the WarehouseManagementSystem reads them with the modules, in the same read. Where it picks the destination, it picks only among the destinations the origin reaches, and refuses the spawn with “no exit module can be reached from Module1” where none is. Where it picks the origin, it picks only among the free origins that reach the named destination, or else some destination, and refuses with “no free entry module can reach Module4”, or “… can reach an exit module”, where free origins exist but none does. A named destination that the named origin cannot reach is refused with “Module4 cannot be reached from Module1”. The reasons name the roles as the others do: “module” where the topology names no such role. Where the origin has no other destination at all, the reason stays “the graph has no exit module other than Module1”.

A strategy picks. The picks above are those of real, the default WMS strategy (strategies): where the request leaves an end to pick, the WarehouseManagementSystem’s strategy picks one pair among the candidates, the free origins with the destinations each reaches, and a pair that is not a candidate refuses the spawn. The strategy is chosen at start in the Launcher, for the WMS and for the modules, and real is the default.

Spawns at once. Spawns run at once, each on the thread of its request or of the spawn timer, and the timer has no wait of its own (spawn_rate). At most as many spawns are under way at once, of the spawn workflow and of the timer alike, as the topology has modules that may be an origin, by the latest read of the modules that a spawn made; before the first read there is no cap. A spawn asked for at the cap does not start, and is refused with “every origin has a spawn under way”. Concurrent spawns pick apart: the origin of a spawn under way is not free for another spawn’s pick, as a module that holds a box is not. The WMS spawns as fast as the hardware allows, with no wait of its own.

The picking lock covers only the slot bookkeeping. A spawn reads the graph outside it: the modules with the connections, and what each origin it may pick holds. Under it, it chooses its origin and its destination in memory, from what it read and from the spawns in flight, and enters itself in flight; it takes the lock again only to leave the flight. So a spawn that has ended frees its slot without waiting for another spawn’s reads, and concurrent spawns still pick apart. A read can be stale: when another spawn left the flight after this spawn’s reads began, its origin may hold that spawn’s box by now, so this spawn reads once more before it chooses. A Reserve that the origin refuses still refuses the spawn with the origin’s reason.

Every spawn accounted for. Each spawn asked for ends in exactly one record at INFO: the record of the box spawned, or “spawn refused: <reason>”, with its origin and destination where they are known (spawn_record). A spawn counts at its answer, created once the store admitted its create: one whose origin then cannot be notified is recorded as spawned, beside its warning. A spawn that raises is recorded as refused with “the spawn failed: <error>”, and its traceback is logged where it was asked: by the spawn timer, or by the middleware for the workflow. Every answer, a refusal at the cap included, goes into its memory. No box is lost silently: each spawn asked for is either created or logged with its reason.

The box is spawned directly on the origin. The WarehouseManagementSystem asks the origin’s Reserve for the box, and writes nothing before the origin accepts. The Reserve names the box’s destination, and the origin accepts only once its own next module has accepted a plain Reserve for the box at once, so no origin fills with a box that cannot move on; a box that waits for the origin comes before the spawn. An origin that holds a box, or has reserved itself for another, refuses, and the spawn is refused with the origin’s reason, whether the request named the origin or the WarehouseManagementSystem picked it: no other module is tried, and the origin is not asked again. An origin that cannot be reached refuses the spawn too, with why (“Module1 is not reachable: …”). So a module that passes a box onto the origin at the same moment, and asks the origin’s Reserve too, is refused as reserved when the WarehouseManagementSystem asked first, and waits its turn. Once the origin has accepted, one mw.create() writes the box, its :BoxTransportOperation as queued and assigned to the origin, the links between the two, and the box’s first possession, by the origin. The store admits or refuses it as one: if it refuses, as it does when a neighbour with skip-reserve armed has filled the origin since its answer, nothing is written, the refusal is logged with its report and emitted on the WarehouseManagementSystem’s handover event stream, and the answer carries the report. Then it notifies the origin of the transport: the origin’s intake reads the transport and carries the box on, and the notification uses the origin’s reservation up. It logs its side, at INFO: the origin’s acceptance of its Reserve, the transport queued, and the spawn’s one record: every spawn ends in one record.

An origin that cannot be notified. The box is on the origin and the transport is assigned to it, but nothing would start its BoxTransportWorkflow: the WarehouseManagementSystem ends the transport failed with why (“Module1 is not reachable: …”), as a module does that cannot reach its next module, logs one warning, and the answer of the spawn says so. The origin leaves that transport alone. So the WarehouseManagementSystem writes a transport only in the create that opens it and when it cannot notify the origin.

A stop. Once the stop of its process has begun, it refuses every spawn, with the reason “the system is stopping”, and creates no box, neither for the spawn workflow nor for the spawn rate’s timer: it asks once more just before the create. A spawn already under way ends. If it cannot notify the origin then, since every process of the run stops at once and the origin’s server takes no new connection, it ends the transport failed with the stop as its reason, “the system is stopping”, and its warning says why the origin was not told; the origin, which waits for the notification at its own stop, writes nothing.

The notification back. The destination module notifies the WarehouseManagementSystem once it has given the box back, and a module that cannot go on notifies it once it has recorded the transport failed: the transport has ended. The WarehouseManagementSystem checks the transport it is handed: it accepts a box it holds whose transport is done, and logs the acceptance as its evidence. Whether it holds the box it asks the store in one ASK about that one box.

What it knows. It keeps in memory the answers to its newest spawns, the transports it opened with their last known status, and the boxes it holds, from the notifications back (wms_memory). Its dashboard renders that memory.

The candidates of a spawn: each of origins from which a path over connections leads to one of destinations other than itself, with those destinations, both in their order. An origin that reaches none is no candidate.

The WarehouseManagementSystem picks its origin among these, and its destination among those of the origin, so that it picks only an exit it can reach from the origin, for every topology. Its WMS strategy picks the pair, and real picks so.

spawn(request: SpawnBoxRequest) → SpawnBoxResult[source]#

Ask the origin’s Reserve for a new box; once the origin has accepted, create the box on it with its transport, and notify it.

The answer is kept in its memory. So is a spawn that raised, as refused with the error as its reason, before the error goes on. Each answer is logged as one record at INFO (spawn_record): the box spawned, or the spawn refused with why, so that no spawn asked for, of the spawn workflow or of the spawn timer, is lost silently.

Spawns under way at once. At most as many spawns are under way at once as the topology has modules that may be an origin, of the spawn workflow and of the spawn timer alike. A spawn asked for at that cap does not start, and is refused with “every origin has a spawn under way”. The WMS spawns as fast as the hardware allows, with no wait of its own.

The notification that transport has ended: accept a box it holds whose transport is done, and log a transport that failed.
