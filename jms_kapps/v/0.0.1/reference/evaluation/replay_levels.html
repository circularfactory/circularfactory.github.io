jms_kapps.evaluation.replay_levels#

The replay levels of the store-only baseline: a recorded run replayed compressed in time, one factor at a time (jms_kapps 98, decided by Sören on 2026-10-12, as the product owner of the tests, jms_kapps 85; interim names, jms_kapps 83). Pure functions: no store, no clock.

A level is one replay of one recorded run at one factor: the same requests in the same order, with every offset from the window’s start, and so every gap between two of them, divided by the factor, and the window as short. A factor of 1 is the recorded timeline, the replay as it has always been. The factors are a setting of a sweep entry of a spec (spec: replay_factors), outside the configuration’s key. A level has a key of its own, the replayed configuration’s key and the factor (level_key).

The rates, both per minute. The offered rate is the number of the replayed window’s requests divided by the window’s length in minutes over the factor. The achieved rate is the number of answered requests, those the store gave an answer, a refusal too and a request that timed out or whose connection dropped not, divided by the level’s replay span in minutes, but the replay span is at least the window: the larger of the span and the window’s length over the factor (jms_kapps 107, decided by Sören on 2026-10-13 as the product owner of the tests, jms_kapps 85, note 1866296, the changed reading “The replay span is read literally”). The span runs from the level’s window start to its last answer, the drain included, and the record keeps it as it is (replay_span_seconds). So the achieved rate is never more than the offered rate, and 100 % means that the store kept up: where every request is answered and the last answer comes before the window’s end, the achieved rate is the offered rate. A level whose last answer comes after the window’s end has the rate it had before. (Until 2026-10-14 the span was read literally, so a fast last answer before the window’s end gave an achieved rate above the offered rate, such as 103 % for a last answer at 290 s of 300.)

Broken (signals): a level is broken when the store refuses a request for want of heap, when more than 1 % of the window’s requests are refused or unanswered, timed out or with their connection dropped (the share of the ramp’s broken rule, ramp; the signal was named refused or timed out until 2026-10-14, jms_kapps 109, for the same sum), or when the achieved rate is below 90 % of the offered rate. A replay has no commits, so the ramp’s commit-latency signal and its plateau do not apply. The thresholds are judgement settings outside the configuration’s key. The sweep stops the replay of a configuration at its first broken level (sweep). The level’s record names the signals that broke it.

Heavy. Every factor above 1 is a heavy run, and runs only under an approval that covers the spec and the replayed configuration (approval). A factor of 1 is a replay at normal load.

jms_kapps.evaluation.replay_levels.level_record(*, factor: float, recorded_window_seconds: float, window_start: datetime, window: Sequence[Sent], refused: int, timed_out: int, heap_refusals: int, connection_dropped: int = 0) → dict[str, Any][source]#

The record of one replay level: its factor, its offered and achieved rates per minute (the answered requests over the larger of the replay span and the window, jms_kapps 107), the refusals, the timeouts and the dropped connections in the window (jms_kapps 109), the refusals for want of heap, the replay span, and whether it is broken, with the signals that broke it.

Parameters:

  • factor – The replay’s factor.

  • recorded_window_seconds – How long the recorded run’s window lasted.

  • window_start – When the replay’s window started, by its clock.

  • window – Every request the replay sent in the window, with its answer or its error.

  • refused – How many of them the store refused.

  • timed_out – How many got no answer because the time ran out.

  • heap_refusals – How many the store refused for want of heap.

  • connection_dropped – How many got no answer because their connection dropped.
