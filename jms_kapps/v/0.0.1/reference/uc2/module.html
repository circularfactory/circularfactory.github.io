jms_kapps.uc2.module#

One FlexConveyorModule’s part in a box’s journey: what it answers, how it carries a box on, and how it gives the box back.

A transport is assigned to one module at a time, the one that carries the box on now, and the assigned module also possesses the box. The WarehouseManagementSystem creates the box on the origin with its transport, assigned to the origin, and notifies the origin. Every later module gets the box and the transport in the handover of the module before it, and goes on once that module calls its Receive: then it reads the box with its transport, through the box’s link to it (fc:hasTransport). (Changed on 2026-10-06, jms_kapps 41, decided by Etienne. Until then the WarehouseManagementSystem “opens the transport assigned to the origin, hands the box to it and notifies it”.)

The BoxTransportWorkflow carries a transport on while the transport is assigned to the module. At the origin, the WarehouseManagementSystem’s notification of the transport starts it: the middleware’s intake hands the module the transport, read once. At every later module, the module’s Receive starts it, with the transport it read through the box. It reads the station’s connections at every decision, in one read, the WarehouseManagementSystem and the next module’s BoxTransportCapability once, and a peer’s workflow endpoint at every call. It acts only when its module is the assigned module, which also holds the box: the two move in one commit, and each handover checks the possession again before it commits. At the origin it commits the queued transport running; the store refuses that while the box is not on the module, and then the transport stays queued (changed on 2026-10-06, jms_kapps 41, decided by Etienne: the WarehouseManagementSystem creates the box on the origin before it notifies the origin, so this happens only to a box that is elsewhere; until then “not on the module yet”). At a later module it goes on with the running one. A transport that is done or has failed is left alone: a failed transport stays failed. Where the module is the destination, it gives the box back to the WarehouseManagementSystem: one handover whose entry makes the WarehouseManagementSystem the possessor and the transport done in one commit, then the move, then a notification of the WarehouseManagementSystem. Otherwise it hops to the first neighbour that its module strategy names (strategies). With real, the default, that is the next module on a shortest path to the destination, by hop count over the connections, a tie going to the lowest IRI, and never straight back to the module the box came from (the build’s reading), unless a crash has left a neighbour out for the box (below); a box goes on to the module that its module reserved ahead for it, while a connection still leads there. No module routes by a table of routes or of neighbours, and nothing routes centrally; the connections it read last it keeps for its dashboard only. (Changed on 2026-10-11, jms_kapps 77, decided by Etienne: “unless a crash has left a neighbour out for the box” was added.) (Changed on 2026-10-06, jms_kapps 43, decided by Etienne: “the box then goes to the module reserved ahead”. Until then the hop’s sentence ended “a tie going to the lowest IRI.”) (Changed on 2026-10-10, jms_kapps 52, decided by Etienne on 2026-10-07: a strategy chooses, real by default. Until then: “Otherwise it hops to the next module on a shortest path to the destination, by hop count over the connections, a tie going to the lowest IRI, and never straight back to the module the box came from (the build’s reading); a box goes on to the module that its module reserved ahead for it, while a connection still leads there.”)

One hop works like the real FlexConveyor. The giving module asks the receiving module’s Reserve. A module reserves itself only while it holds nothing, has promised itself to no other box, and no box that has waited longer still asks for it, in its memory, and answers at once. Its read of what it holds and its reservation are one step, which a Receive waits for: otherwise a Reserve that read an empty module could be accepted just after the box it was reserved for arrived. The graph still refuses a second box on a module, whatever the modules believed. A module that refuses with a refusal that passes says so in its answer (occupied, reserved, behind or blocked), and the giving module asks its detour once, and then waits the retry time and asks again, with no bound, holding the box meanwhile. Any other refusal ends the transport, once the detour has refused too, but “no live route on”, the refusal of a module whose look-ahead found no live module left: the giving module leaves that module out, as one it cannot reach (below). (Changed on 2026-10-11, jms_kapps 77, decided by Etienne: “no live route on”. Until then the sentence ended “once the detour has refused too.”) Once the receiving module has accepted, the giving module opens the middleware’s handover: its entry commits the change of possession and assigns the transport to the receiving module, and the store admits or refuses both together, before anything moves. Only once the store has admitted it does the giving module convey the box: its own move, a function the module is given, and no workflow that a module exposes. Then it calls the receiving module’s Receive, the receiving module’s light barrier, which never refuses, because the possession has already changed. The receiving module answers, and then, as its own work, reads the box with its transport and carries the box on. So a hop is two calls between the modules, Reserve and Receive, and one commit, and no notification passes between them, and no module polls the graph for a box it is about to receive. The receiving module logs each step it accepts, so its own log shows that the step ran there. The giving module logs its side, at INFO too: the receiving module’s answer to its Reserve, and its convey once the box has gone. So each module’s activity page shows the hop from both ends. (Changed on 2026-10-06, jms_kapps 43, decided by Etienne. Until then: “A module reserves itself only while it holds nothing and has promised itself to no other box” and “A module that refuses as occupied or reserved says so in its answer, and the giving module waits the retry time and asks again, with no bound, holding the box meanwhile.”)

Routing keeps boxes flowing (decided by Etienne on 2026-10-06, jms_kapps 43):

  • Look-ahead acceptance. A module accepts a Reserve only if it is free and either it is the box’s destination, or its own next module accepts a plain Reserve for the box at once; that inner ask looks no further ahead. Otherwise it refuses at once, as blocked, and keeps no reservation. The box then goes on to the module reserved ahead, whose reservation is bound by ARRIVAL_BOUND_SECONDS and two times T (ahead_bound), T the longest transit time of any module. So a box moves only onto a module from which it can move on at once, and no module fills with a box that cannot. It routes around a crash too: a next module it cannot reach it leaves out for the box, and it looks ahead to the next one its strategy names, the module that holds the box still excluded; where no live module is left, it refuses “no live route on”, which does not pass, and the asker leaves it out as one it cannot reach. (Added on 2026-10-11, jms_kapps 77, decided by Etienne.)

  • One detour before waiting. When the next module declines, the giving module asks one other neighbour, the second its module strategy names; with real (routing.detour), not the module the box came from, and one from which the destination is reached without coming back, so the module the next module ties with (changed on 2026-10-10, jms_kapps 52, decided by Etienne on 2026-10-07: a strategy chooses, real by default; until then “asks one other neighbour (routing.detour): not the module the box came from, and one from which the destination is reached without coming back. The module the next module ties with is the detour.”). Only if it declines too does the module wait and ask both again, or end the transport where the next module’s refusal does not pass. Once a crash has left a neighbour out, the strategy is asked with no module the box came from, so the detour, or the first, may name the module the box came from (added on 2026-10-11, jms_kapps 77, decided by Etienne: “The module the box came from is now a valid choice.”). The module reserved ahead looks ahead again when the box asks it, and may refuse then, as blocked (the build’s reading).

  • Longest-waiting first. A module that is free serves the box that has waited longest (WaitingOrder): any other box is refused as behind. A box keeps its place, the inner ask of a look-ahead included, and the WarehouseManagementSystem’s Reserve waits behind every box. A box leaves the order once it arrives, moves elsewhere or its transport ends, which the module reads in the graph when it would otherwise refuse a box; a box that has stopped asking for a while comes first no more, but keeps its place.

  • The stop. Once the stop of its process has begun, the module asks no Reserve, neither at its look-ahead nor of its detour, as the stop rules say.

  • `skip-reserve` makes the module skip the look-ahead and the detour for its own hop: it accepts a box from a module without looking ahead, and hands it on without asking, so on a busy station it meets an occupied module, and the store refuses it. The WarehouseManagementSystem’s Reserve is always looked ahead for.

A reservation lapses at its bound. A box the module reserved itself for that has not arrived within ARRIVAL_BOUND_SECONDS and T, the longest transit time of any module in the graph (read at the process’s start), or two T for a box reserved ahead, counted from the module’s answer to Reserve, makes the module read the graph, and the box leaves its waiting order (both added on 2026-10-06, jms_kapps 43, decided by Etienne). If the box is its own already and its transport has not ended, the giving module was lost after its commit and before its Receive: the module ends the transport failed, with a reason that names the giving module. Otherwise it frees its reservation. A transport that has ended is left alone. So after a commit the store refused, the giving module keeps the box and ends the transport failed with the store’s reason, and the receiving module’s reservation lapses at its bound. A box handed over with skip-reserve armed comes with no reservation, so no bound: if its giving module is lost after the commit, the transport stays running.

The box of a spawn is created on the origin. The WarehouseManagementSystem asks the origin’s Reserve, creates the box on the origin with its transport, and notifies the origin. The notification takes the place of a Receive: the middleware’s intake reads the transport, and the BoxTransportWorkflow carries the box on, which uses the reservation up. A reservation for the WarehouseManagementSystem’s box lapses ARRIVAL_BOUND_SECONDS after the answer to Reserve, with no transit time, since no move comes before the notification. At that bound the module frees the reservation and writes nothing: the WarehouseManagementSystem owns the station’s entry, and ends the transport itself when it cannot notify the origin. So the module leaves a transport that has ended alone, and one that has not to the WarehouseManagementSystem; the second is the build’s reading, where the ticket names only the first. (Changed on 2026-10-06, jms_kapps 41, decided by Etienne. Until then the paragraph read: “Until the spawn changes, the WarehouseManagementSystem’s handover onto the origin keeps its handshake: the origin that has reserved itself for the WarehouseManagementSystem’s box calls the WarehouseManagementSystem’s Convey, which opens the transport, hands the box over and calls the origin’s Receive. That Receive starts nothing: the WarehouseManagementSystem’s notification of the transport starts the origin. Such a reservation needs no bound of its own, since the call of Convey has a timeout.”)

(Changed on 2026-10-06, jms_kapps 40, decided by Etienne. Until then the receiving module called the giving module’s Convey once it had accepted Reserve, Convey opened the handover, moved the box and called Receive, and the paragraph on the hop went on: “Its body is the move, a function the module is given, and then the receiving module’s Receive, which only confirms the arrival: it never refuses, because the possession has already changed, and it starts nothing. After the handover the giving module notifies the receiving module of the transport, and that notification starts the receiving module’s BoxTransportWorkflow.”)

A module that cannot go on commits the transport failed, with the reason and the store’s report where there is one, and notifies the WarehouseManagementSystem. Only the assigned module writes its transport.

A next module that cannot be reached before the handover, because the graph names no live workflow of it, or the call of one is refused, fails or times out, is left out for the box: the module logs one warning that names it (“Module9: leaves Module15 out for Box1: Module15 is not reachable: …”), raises nothing, keeps the box, and asks its strategy again, with the connections to each module left out removed and with no module the box came from, which is then a valid choice. Then the hop goes on as any hop: the first, then the detour, and the wait while both refuse with a refusal that passes, with no time limit. A module left out stays out while the box is here, and so does a detour that cannot be reached. A next module that refuses “no live route on” is left out the same way. Only where no neighbour is left to ask does the module end the transport the same way, with a reason that names the destination and each module left out (“no live route to Module9: Module3 and Module1 are not reachable”). With skip-reserve armed, a hop the store refused asks first, and leaves no module out: skip-reserve onto a crashed module stays as it was (the build’s reading of jms_kapps 77, whose ticket leaves it out of scope). (Changed on 2026-10-11, jms_kapps 77, decided by Etienne: “When the next module is not reachable, the giving module leaves it out for this box and chooses again”, and “The transport ends failed only when no neighbour is left to ask”. Until then: “A next module that cannot be reached before the handover ends the transport the same way, with “Module3 is not reachable: …” and why as its reason: the graph names no live workflow of it, or the call of one is refused, fails or times out. The module logs one warning, raises nothing, and keeps the box.”) After the handover, when the next module’s Receive cannot reach that module, the box is on it and the transport is assigned to it: the module that passed both on writes nothing, and says why in one warning. The receiving module ends the transport: at the bound of its reservation, or at its stop. A receiving module that crashed after the commit ends it at its next start, when the middleware’s crash recovery ends every running Operation assigned to it. A module finds the transport of a box it holds through the box, which links to it (fc:hasTransport): it reads the box with its transport in one fetch, and no rule names a transport after its box. (Changed on 2026-10-06, jms_kapps 42, decided by Etienne: only the assigned module writes the transport. Until then the module that passed both on ended the transport failed, “the one case where a module writes a transport not assigned to it. This is what a stop does to a transport in flight, and what a peer that crashes during a run does”.) (Changed on 2026-10-06, jms_kapps 41, decided by Etienne. Until then the paragraph went on: “An origin that cannot reach the WarehouseManagementSystem’s Convey releases its reservation and says why […] Unless the WarehouseManagementSystem committed the handover before it was lost: then the box and the transport are the origin’s, and no notification of the transport will come, so the origin ends it failed.”)

The deadlock alarm. The module measures how long each box it holds waits for a Reserve, from its first ask of one. Past deadlock_alarm_seconds, the launch’s deadlock alarm time, 300 s unless a run sets another (–deadlock-alarm-seconds), it raises the alarm once for the box, before its next wait: one WARNING line that starts with “possible deadlock:” and names the module, the box, the time waited and the modules it asks, and one deadlock_alarm event on its handover event stream. The box keeps waiting, and nothing fails: the alarm is the visible backstop for boxes on a cycle of connections that wait for each other. It writes nothing to the graph. (Added on 2026-10-11, jms_kapps 77, decided by Etienne.)

A stop. From the stop signal on, the module waits at most the graceful-shutdown bound for the box it reserved itself for and for its own work: a move under way finishes, and the giving module calls Receive. Its server takes no new connection by then, so only a Receive already under way still comes. If no Receive came, and the box is its own with its transport not ended, its giving module committed the handover: the module ends the transport failed, “the system is stopping”. The origin of a spawn waits for the WarehouseManagementSystem’s notification instead, and leaves the transport of the WarehouseManagementSystem’s box to it, which owns the station’s entry. (Added on 2026-10-06, jms_kapps 42, decided by Etienne.) Once the stop has begun, the module also refuses every Reserve, asks none, and hands no box to another module: the module a box’s transport is assigned to keeps the box and ends the transport failed, “the system is stopping”. A destination still gives its box back to the WarehouseManagementSystem, with the transport done. (Added on 2026-10-06, jms_kapps 42: the build’s reading of Etienne’s decision that only the assigned module writes the transport, where the ticket names no rule for a hop not committed yet.)

Two faults, §5.2’s faults of a module’s own logic, are armed at run time and off when the module starts. What stops either is the graph alone, and each refusal is logged with its report and emitted on the module’s handover event stream.

  • skip-reserve makes the giving module skip Reserve and commit the handover regardless. Onto a module that holds a box, the store refuses that, nothing moves, and the module adapts: it asks Reserve first, the proper way, and waits its turn while the receiving module is occupied or reserved, so the box still arrives. Onto a free module, it conveys the box and calls Receive, and the receiving module carries the box on.

  • complete-without-releasing makes the destination commit the transport done before it gives the box back. The store refuses that while the module holds the box, and the module then gives the box back properly, with the last handover.

This is the adaptation the paper describes, hardcoded. A module that reads the report and decides for itself is future work.

Only a ShaclValidationError is the graph’s refusal, and its report travels verbatim. Any other error of the store, such as a dataset that does not exist or a read that failed, is not caught.

  • later – Runs this module’s own work after some seconds: the bound of a reservation.

  • arrival_bound – How long a box the module reserved itself for may take to arrive, counted from its answer to Reserve: ARRIVAL_BOUND_SECONDS and T, the longest transit time of any module. The WarehouseManagementSystem’s notification of a box that the module, as the origin of a spawn, reserved itself for may take SPAWN_BOUND_SECONDS.

  • ahead_bound – How long a box the module reserved itself for ahead, at the plain Reserve of a look-ahead, may take to arrive: ARRIVAL_BOUND_SECONDS and two T, since the box first moves onto the module that asked.

  • clock – The time, in seconds, by which the waiting order counts how long a box waited.

  • events – The module’s handover event stream, where a refused handover is emitted.

  • memory – What the module keeps of its own work, for its dashboard.

  • stopping – Whether the stop of the module’s process has begun.

  • strategy – The module strategy that answers which neighbours the module asks for a box, real unless a run chooses another (strategies).

  • strategy_name – Its name, which a warning about one of its answers names.

  • deadlock_alarm_seconds – How long a box the module holds may wait for a Reserve, counted from its first ask of one, before the module raises the deadlock alarm for it, by the clock clock. (Added on 2026-10-11, jms_kapps 77, decided by Etienne.)

box_transport(transport: Transport, *, came_from: IRI | None = None, ahead: IRI | None = None) → None[source]#

The BoxTransportWorkflow: carry the box of transport on, while the transport is assigned to this module.

came_from is the module the box came from, which it does not go straight back to, and ahead the next module that reserved itself for the box at this module’s look-ahead, where the box goes on to; a reservation this module made for the box names it too. (Added on 2026-10-06, jms_kapps 43, decided by Etienne.)

It acts on the transport it is handed: at the origin, as the middleware’s intake read it for the WarehouseManagementSystem’s notification; at a later module, as its Receive read it through the box. Either way it carries the box on the same way. A transport that is done or has failed, or is assigned to another module, is logged and left alone. Once the module acts on a transport assigned to it, a reservation it made for the box is used up. The transport alone does not tell the module that it possesses the box, so its memory does not hold the box for it.

At the origin, the queued transport is committed running first. The store refuses that while the box is not on the module: then the transport stays queued, and the module writes nothing more and notifies no one. The WarehouseManagementSystem creates the box on the origin before it notifies the origin, so that happens only to a box that is elsewhere. (Changed on 2026-10-06, jms_kapps 41, decided by Etienne. Until then: “while the box is not on the module yet: then the transport stays queued, because the WarehouseManagementSystem holds the box and writes its transport”.) A running transport whose box is held elsewhere fails at the next handover, which checks the possession before it commits.

It counts as the module’s own work, which a stop of its process waits for.

carry_on(transport: Transport, *, came_from: IRI | None = None, ahead: IRI | None = None) → None[source]#

Carry the box of transport toward its destination: give it back there, or start the hop to the first neighbour that the module’s strategy names, with came_from, the module the box came from, and ahead, the module reserved ahead for it at this module’s look-ahead (strategies); the second it names is the detour, which the module tries when the first declines. A module that cannot go on says why in the transport. (Changed on 2026-10-06, jms_kapps 43, decided by Etienne: the box goes on to the module reserved ahead, and a declined Reserve leads to one detour. Until then: “or start the hop to the next module on a shortest path to it.”) (Changed on 2026-10-10, jms_kapps 52, decided by Etienne on 2026-10-07: a strategy chooses, real by default, which is the choice made here until then. Until then: “or start the hop to the next module on a shortest path to it, which never runs straight back to came_from. The module reserved ahead for the box comes first while a connection still leads there. The detour is the one other module it tries when the next module declines (routing.detour).”)

A crash is routed around. A neighbour it asks that cannot be reached, or that refuses “no live route on”, it leaves out for the box, with one warning that names it, and it chooses again: its strategy is asked again with the connections to each module left out removed, and with no module the box came from, which is then a valid choice. A module left out stays out while the box is here. Only where no neighbour is left to ask does the transport end failed, with a reason that names the destination and each module left out. The choice again decides on the connections read for this decision, and reads none. (Added on 2026-10-11, jms_kapps 77, decided by Etienne: “When the next module is not reachable, the giving module leaves it out for this box and chooses again. The module the box came from is now a valid choice.”)

hop(transport: Transport, receiver: IRI, detour_to: IRI | None = None, *, choose_again: Callable[[Sequence[IRI]], list[IRI]] | None = None) → None[source]#

The hop of the box of transport to receiver: ask its Reserve, and once it has accepted, commit the handover, convey the box and call its Receive. While receiver refuses, ask detour_to once, and take the hop there if it accepts; otherwise wait and ask both again, or, where the refusal of receiver does not pass, end the transport.

With choose_again, a module asked that cannot be reached, or that refuses “no live route on”, is left out for the box, and choose_again names the neighbours to ask then (added on 2026-10-11, jms_kapps 77, decided by Etienne). Without it, as before, such a receiver ends the transport, and such a detour is asked no more.

With skip-reserve armed, commit the handover onto receiver without asking, and so without a detour. If the store refuses that, ask first, as the hop should have, and wait its turn the same way, with no module left out: skip-reserve onto a crashed module stays as it was (the build’s reading of jms_kapps 77, whose ticket leaves it out of scope). (Changed on 2026-10-06, jms_kapps 43, decided by Etienne: one detour before waiting. Until then: “While receiver refuses as occupied or reserved, wait and ask again.”)

give_back(transport: Transport) → None[source]#

Give the box of transport back to the WarehouseManagementSystem, and notify it.

The handover’s entry makes the WarehouseManagementSystem the possessor and the transport done in one commit, so no moment has a box on a module with its transport done, or a box back in the WMS with its transport running.

cannot_go_on(transport: Transport, reason: str, report: str | None = None) → None[source]#

End transport as failed with reason, and notify the WarehouseManagementSystem.

This module is the one the transport is assigned to: it is handed the transport only then, and it keeps it until the box has gone. The box stays here.

reserve(request: ReserveRequest) → ReserveResult[source]#

Reserve this module for a box, in its memory, and set the reservation’s bound: for a box of the WarehouseManagementSystem, whose notification comes with no move before it, SPAWN_BOUND_SECONDS, for a box reserved ahead, at the plain Reserve of a look-ahead, ahead_bound, and for a box of a module arrival_bound. (Changed on 2026-10-06, jms_kapps 41, decided by Etienne. Until then “The origin of a spawn, reserved for the WarehouseManagementSystem’s box, calls the WarehouseManagementSystem’s Convey instead”.)

Look-ahead acceptance. The module accepts only while it holds nothing, has promised itself to no other box, and no box that has waited longer still asks (WaitingOrder); and then only where it is the box’s destination, or its own next module, which it asks a plain Reserve for the box, accepts at once. Otherwise it refuses at once and keeps no reservation. A plain Reserve, and a Reserve that names no destination, looks no further ahead. Every box but the WarehouseManagementSystem’s takes its place in the waiting order at its first ask, whatever the answer. With skip-reserve armed, the module looks ahead for the WarehouseManagementSystem’s Reserve alone. (Added on 2026-10-06, jms_kapps 43, decided by Etienne.)

Once the stop of the module’s process has begun, it refuses, with the reason “the system is stopping” and no refusal that passes, and sets no bound: a reservation then would outlast the module. (Added on 2026-10-06, jms_kapps 42: the build’s reading of Etienne’s decision that only the assigned module writes the transport.)

receive(request: ReceiveRequest) → ReceiveResult[source]#

The light barrier: a box has arrived. The possession has changed already, so it never refuses. Once it has answered, it reads the box with its transport and carries the box on, as its own work. (Changed on 2026-10-06, jms_kapps 41, decided by Etienne: the WarehouseManagementSystem calls no Receive. Until then “A Receive from the WarehouseManagementSystem starts nothing”.)

stop(within: float) → None[source]#

This module’s part in the stop of its process, which has begun: wait at most within seconds for the box it reserved itself for and for its own work, then decide on what is left of its reservation. It returns when it has.

  • A box from a module: once the stop has begun, the module’s server takes no new connection, so only a Receive already under way still comes, and uses the reservation up. If none has come by then, the module reads the graph: if the box is its own, the giving module committed the handover, and the module, now the assigned module, ends the transport failed, “the system is stopping”, unless it has ended. A box that is not here it frees, and writes nothing.

  • The box of a spawn, at the origin: the WarehouseManagementSystem’s notification, not a Receive, uses the reservation up. If none has come, the module frees the reservation and writes nothing: the WarehouseManagementSystem owns the station’s entry, and ends the transport itself when it cannot notify the origin.

  • Its own work: a hop in its move finishes the move and calls Receive, and work that has not handed its box on yet ends its transport failed, “the system is stopping”, at its next step, since the module asks no Reserve and hands no box to another module once the stop has begun (the build’s reading); a destination gives its box back.

(Added on 2026-10-06, jms_kapps 42, decided by Etienne: inside the station only the assigned module writes the transport.)
