jms_kapps.evaluation.observation#

What the harness reads of a run once its participants have stopped: the measures of its record and the checks that make the run invalid. It reads the spans that the wrapper wrote for each participant (instrumentation), the completion matcher’s reads (matcher), the graph’s transports after the drain, and each participant’s output, each line stamped with the time it arrived.

The measures. Every store request of every participant, with its time, its latency, its size and its outcome: accepted; refused, with the store’s reason; timed out or connection dropped, with the error of its client (store_requests.unanswered_outcome). Each commit of the object-graph mapper, with its latency and its outcome. Then, over the measured window only, from the warm-up’s end to the window’s end: the latencies of the commits that the store admitted; the completed writes per minute, the writes the store applied, each checked write counted once, by its update and not by the removal of its receipt; the store requests that the store refused, that timed out and whose connection dropped, and the share of the three together (refused or unanswered); the refusals for want of heap; and the transports ended per minute, the ends the matcher saw in the window. The record keeps every store request with its time, those of the participants’ start and of the warm-up too, because the store-only baseline replays them; only the latencies, rates and shares count the window alone. A run without the wrapper has no spans: its measures are those that need none, the transports ended per minute, beside the matcher’s reads, the graph’s transports after the drain and the refused spawns that the record holds anyway.

The checks. A run that fails one is invalid, and its record names the check:

  • one accepted done or failed: each Operation that the graph shows ended after the drain has exactly one accepted write of done or failed;

  • at most one accepted running: and at most one of running;

  • no done before its running: and no accepted done before an accepted running. An Operation may have no accepted running at all, as “at most one” allows: a transport that the WarehouseManagementSystem ends failed while it is still queued has none, and a running whose answer timed out is not known to be accepted. Either is valid.

  • the matcher saw the graph’s ends: the transports that the matcher saw end are the transports that the graph shows ended after the drain. The matcher reads once more after the graph’s last read, at the drain’s end, so a transport that the graph read showed still open and that ended just after it is seen ended by the matcher alone, and is no failure.

  • deadlock alarm: a module raised a deadlock alarm between the load’s start and the drain’s end: a line of its output whose log message starts with “possible deadlock:”, after the level and the logger that the participants’ log format writes before it. The check names the module and the line as it was written, which names the modules asked; nothing after “possible deadlock:” is read. A line that arrived before the load’s start or after the drain’s end does not count.

An accepted write is one that the store accepted and applied: a checked write whose receipt the store did not find wrote nothing (store_requests). A write that the store refused, and that its participant sent again, counts once, as the accepted one. The first three checks need the spans, so a run without the wrapper makes only the last two; the record lists the checks it made. An Operation still open after the drain counts as stuck, with its place, and that alone does not make a run invalid.

jms_kapps.evaluation.observation.request_measures(window: Window, requests: Sequence[Mapping[str, Any]]) → dict[str, Any][source]#

The measures of requests, store requests as the record holds them, over the window alone: how many there were, how many the store refused, how many timed out and how many lost their connection, and the share of the three, refused or unanswered; the completed writes, each checked write counted once, by its update and not by the removal of its receipt, and their number per minute of window; and the refusals for want of heap, each with whether it came in the window. A UC2 run’s measures hold them, and so do the store-only baseline’s (baseline).

The checks of the statuses written for each Operation that the graph shows ended after the drain, graph_ends as the record holds the run’s transports: each check that fails, with the Operation and its accepted writes of a status.

jms_kapps.evaluation.observation.matcher_check(matcher: Mapping[str, Any], graph_ends: Mapping[str, Any]) → list[dict[str, Any]][source]#

Whether the transports that the matcher saw end are those that the graph shows ended after the drain: none where they are, else one failed check that names those it saw alone and those the graph shows alone. A transport that the graph’s read showed open and that the matcher’s last read, which comes after it, saw ended, is no failure.

jms_kapps.evaluation.observation.deadlock_alarms(outputs: Mapping[str, Sequence[tuple[datetime | None, str]]], start: datetime, end: datetime) → list[dict[str, Any]][source]#

Each deadlock alarm in outputs, each participant’s stamped output by its resource’s local name, that arrived from start, the load’s start, to end, the drain’s end: a failed check each, with the module, the line as it was written, and when it arrived.

jms_kapps.evaluation.observation.observe(record: Mapping[str, Any], *, spans: Path | None, outputs: Mapping[str, Sequence[tuple[datetime | None, str]]]) → dict[str, Any][source]#

What a completed run’s record gains from its observation: its measures, whether it is valid, the checks it failed and those it made, and its stuck Operations.

Parameters:

  • record – The run’s record, with its warm-up, its window, its actions, its transports after the drain and its matcher.

  • spans – The folder of the participants’ spans, or None for a run without the wrapper.

  • outputs – Each participant’s stamped output, by its resource’s local name.
